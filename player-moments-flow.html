⚽Player Moments — Feature Map

What a player profile does, what it changes in every other portal, and the exact points where a parent decides. Read this alongside the four clickable mockups linked at the bottom.
1 · The idea in one sentence

A player from age ten keeps a private, Instagram-shaped record of their own football life — always visible to a parent, never visible to anyone the parent has not approved — and the guides who actually train them can see it and answer, on the record, where the parent reads every word.

The one rule the whole feature hangs on.

The parent sets the boundary. Inside that boundary, the player decides. Neither of them can hide a moment from the parent, and there is no private channel to a child anywhere on the platform.

3 · The life of one moment
From the player's phone to the guide's screen. Every branch here is a decision someone has to make in the build.
1
Player
Captures a photo or a short video and writes what happened

Optionally tags the session, a guide, or one of their goals. Sixty-second limit on video.

2
Player
Chooses who sees it

The parent chip is pre-ticked and cannot be unticked. Approved guides are pre-ticked according to the player's defaults, and the player can untick any of them for this one post. Nobody outside the approved circle can be added from this screen.

3
Automatic check — runs on every moment in every mode
Media and language screen

Strips location data. Looks for an unrecognised face, a school crest, a street sign, a venue name, and a caption the language model rates as distressed.

CleanContinues to step 4.
FlaggedHeld before anyone sees it. Parent is told it is held and why — except for a tone flag.
Tone flagHeld, parent told it is held, reason withheld from the automated message. A named person calls the parent. The child is told nothing.
4
Parent's review mode decides what happens next
Approve first, or post first
Approve firstHeld for the parent. Guides see nothing until the parent approves.
Post first, review afterGoes straight to the approved guides. Parent can take it down at any time.
Family onlyNever leaves the family, whatever the player picked. A private journal.
5
Parent — on a held moment
Five choices, not two

Approve and share · keep it between us · blur the face and then share · send it back with a note · remove it. "Send it back with a note" is the one that teaches something; a bare reject teaches nothing.

6
Guide
Sees it, reacts, comments

Only guides who have completed a real session with this player, are approved by the parent, and were included by the player on this post. A guide cannot search for players, request access, or follow anyone.

7
System — the instant the guide hits send
The comment is copied to the parent and written to the audit log

Not a digest, not a daily summary. Immediately, in the parent's inbox. The guide sees "copied to the parent" on their own comment as they write it. This is what makes guide-to-player contact defensible.

8
Parent — at any time afterwards
Take down, narrow, report

A take-down removes the moment from every guide immediately and tells the player it happened. We never remove a child's post behind their back — that is how a child learns the system is honest rather than arbitrary.

The default follows the age; the setting does not. A parent can put a ten-year-old on post-first or a fifteen-year-old on family-only. That is what you asked for, and it is also the honest position — parents know their own child and a birthday is a poor proxy for judgement.
7 · Play promotion links — the only outward-facing route
Decided 21 August 2026: there is no public profile. Nothing on this platform produces a findable page carrying a child's name, age, position and photographs. When someone outside the circle needs to see a player, a parent sends one private, expiring link.

What a link is

A parent types a name and a reason, picks an expiry of 7, 30 or 90 days, and gets a link. The recipient's name is printed across every page of it, so a forwarded link is obvious. Every open is logged with a date and shown to the parent and to the player — the child always knows who has looked at their football. Revoking kills it instantly, mid-session. Search engines never index it and it cannot be guessed.

Who it is forThe situationTypical settings
A scout or a clubA trial enquiry, or a club a family has chosen to approach.30 days · passcode on · report summary on if the family made the approach, off if the club did
A current guide outside the circleA guide brought in for one camp or one assessment who has not yet completed a session, so the moments feed is not open to them.30 days · no report · watch-only
A former guideSomeone who trained the player last season and wants to see how they are getting on. Feed access ends when the working relationship does; a link is how the door stays politely open.7 or 30 days · no report · a handful of moments
FamilyGrandparents abroad, a parent who is not on the account.90 days · no report · renewable

What the holder sees

  • First name and initial only — never a surname, on any link, with no setting to change it.
  • Age group and position · badges and streak · up to six moments the player proposes and the parent approves one by one.
  • Player Report summary — off by default. Sensible for a scout the family approached; not for one who approached the family.
  • Parent contact details — off by default, so enquiries come back through the platform rather than to a personal phone.
  • Never shown, with no setting available: school · club · home city · training venue and times · any photo containing another child · anything from a family-only moment · anything the player did not put forward themselves.
Why this is better than a public page, not just safer.

A public profile is a shop window that anyone can walk past. A promotion link is a document a parent hands to a named person for a stated reason, with a clock on it. It gets a family the whole of the actual benefit — a coach who wanted to see the kid play can now see the kid play — while removing every part of the risk. It is also the honest answer to a parent who asks "who can see my child?": a list of names, with dates.

One thing to watch.

A link is a copy of a child's football life leaving your control. Watermarking and expiry raise the cost of misuse; they do not make it impossible. Keep the default moment count low, keep the report off by default, and treat a request to remove the expiry as a signal rather than a feature request.

9 · What this costs — decide before any code
The screens are the cheap part.

Everything below is a real obligation the moment a ten-year-old uploads a video, and none of it is optional.

#DecisionWhy it blocks
1Legal review of a social feature for children, Canada and Croatia togetherDifferent rules, different mandatory reporting routes, one product. Promotion links are lighter than a public page but still put a child's image outside the platform.
2Age assurance and how parental consent is capturedGDPR Article 8 — Croatia sets the age at 16, Canada differs. Determines the whole tier model.

11 · Still open — for David
  • Q1 · Does a player account cost anything? Free with an active program, or part of a subscription that survives between programs? It is the strongest retention asset you have in the off-season.
  • Q2 · Guide response time. If a guide answering a moment is the main hook, what is the promise — same day, forty-eight hours, best effort? It affects guide compensation.
  • Q3 · Does the moment feed touch the Motion Intelligence Platform? Player-uploaded video is training data. If it feeds the Brain, that needs saying explicitly in the consent, and it interacts with the ownership question already open in the partnership file.
  • Q4 · Siblings. Mia is nine. Does she get a read-only view of her brother's timeline, or nothing at all until ten?
  • Q5 · Two parents, one child. Which parent approves? Both, either, or a nominated one? Separated families make this a real question rather than a detail.
  • Q6 · Croatia versus Canada launch. Croatian families arrive through Grego. If the age of consent differs, does the feature launch in one country first?
  • Q7 · Who may be sent a promotion link? Anyone the parent names, or only from a list the platform recognises — a registered guide, a verified club? Free-typing a name is simpler and puts the judgement with the parent; a verified list is safer and much more work.
  • Q8 · Does a former guide keep anything at all? The design says feed access ends with the working relationship and a link is the polite door. Confirm that is what you want, because guides may expect otherwise.
