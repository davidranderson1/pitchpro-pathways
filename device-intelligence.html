Premium feature

The combined Athlete State radar is the headline premium feature.

Why a radar and not six bar charts. A radar is the right chart when you have six or more axes on one common scale and the reader needs the pattern, not the ranking. It is the wrong chart for comparing two unrelated quantities, for more than about eight axes, or when the axis order is arbitrary — order changes the shape, so the order has to mean something. Ours is deliberate: technical and physical on the right, mechanics at the bottom, recovery on the left. A shape leaning left is a tired athlete; a shape leaning right is an athlete outrunning their own body.
Manufacturer collaboration

Three Data PartnersAccess status verified 21 Aug 2026

What each manufacturer actually measures, and what it would take to get that data into the Brain. The access column is the honest one — only one of the three publishes a developer interface anyone can sign up for today.

Partnership required Playermaker

Boot-mounted motion sensor

Straps to both boots. Classifies every ball touch and every step — around 25 technical and physical metrics including ball involvement, two-footed use, release time, kicking velocity, top speed, sprints, accelerations and decelerations.

Access: no public developer documentation found. There is precedent for manufacturer-to-platform data sharing — Playermaker performance data has been integrated into Pixellot's automated video analytics — so the route is a commercial data-sharing agreement, not a self-serve key.

Public API v2 Ōura

Smart ring

Photoplethysmography, body temperature, blood-oxygen and motion sensing overnight. Daily Sleep, Activity, Readiness, Stress and Resilience scores, each broken into contributors already normalised 1–100.

Access: documented REST API version 2 with personal access tokens and OAuth — endpoints for daily readiness, daily sleep, daily activity, daily stress and more. The one device we could prototype against this week.

Partnership required Plantiga

Sensor insole

Sits under the insole of any shoe or boot. Impact and push-off asymmetry, ground contact time, total g-load, mechanical load variability, movement classification (walk, run, jump, cut, accelerate, decelerate) and distance by intensity band.

Access: no public developer documentation found on the site. Positioned explicitly as the layer that fills the gap between motion capture, force plates and GPS — which is exactly the Brain's argument, and the reason they are the most natural partner of the three.

⚠ The honest limit of this design

Three devices measure three different things at three different sampling rates, and none of them was validated against the others. A percentile built on top of them is a modelling choice, not a measurement. Two rules keep it defensible: never draw an axis the source device cannot actually support, and always show the reader which device an axis came from. The moment the radar stops being traceable to a named sensor it becomes decoration.

Blockers, not to-dos

Read This Before Any Code

Nothing here is a design opinion. Each one either stops the feature or changes its shape, and each was found while checking the three manufacturers rather than assumed.

⚠ BLOCKER 1 — Ōura's terms require the account holder to be 18

Ōura's Terms of Use state: “You represent and warrant that you are at least 18 years of age and have the legal authority to accept this Agreement on your behalf or on behalf of any party you represent.” A thirteen-year-old cannot hold an Ōura account. That does not necessarily kill the integration — a parent-held account, or a written arrangement with Ōura, may be the route — but it must be settled with Ōura directly before a single line of integration code is written, and before any recovery axis is shown on a youth profile. Verified against Ōura's own terms, 21 August 2026.

⚠ BLOCKER 2 — two of the three have no public developer interface

Only Ōura publishes an API anyone can build against today. Playermaker and Plantiga would each need a commercial data-sharing agreement. That is a business-development timeline measured in months, not a sprint — and it means the honest build order is Ōura first as a working proof, then walk into the other two conversations with a demonstration rather than a slide.

Open questions for David and Sandro

  • D1 Continuous wearable data on a minor is a different consent conversation from a one-off screening. Canada and Croatia both — and this sits directly on top of the age-assurance blocker already logged against the player-moments feature.
  • D2 Who owns a percentile? The norm set is Sandro's methodology applied to a manufacturer's raw data. The ownership question in the partnership file has to be answered before the first norm table is built on top of third-party data.
  • D3 Does a flagged axis create a duty to act? A radar that shows an injury risk to a parent, and is then ignored, is a different legal object from one that no one saw. Name who is accountable for a flag before the flag exists.
  • D4 Which device is mandatory for a premium plan to mean anything? A premium subscription that shows three empty radars because the family never bought a ring is a refund conversation.
  • D5 Do device metrics feed the Brain's norm tables — that is, does the platform learn from customer data? If yes, it belongs in the consent and in the ownership agreement, and it is the same open question already raised about player-uploaded video.
